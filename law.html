Chapter I

Oath and Department Conduct

“A badge is not worn for authority, but for responsibility.”
1.1

The Deputy’s Oath

Every sworn deputy accepts a duty to protect the people of Ash Creek, uphold the law fairly and serve the territory with integrity. The badge must never be used for personal gain, intimidation or revenge.

1.2

Professional Conduct

Deputies must conduct themselves with professionalism while on duty and while representing the Sheriff’s Office. Members are expected to remain calm, respectful and reasonable, even when dealing with difficult or hostile individuals.

1.3

Roleplay Comes First

Law enforcement exists to create meaningful roleplay for everyone involved. Deputies should focus on developing scenes, investigations and consequences rather than rushing toward arrests or trying to win every encounter.

1.4

Fair and Impartial Policing

All individuals must be treated fairly regardless of their character’s reputation, occupation, relationships or previous dealings with law enforcement. Personal disagreements must not influence official decisions.

1.5

Use of Authority

Deputies may only use the authority granted by their position for legitimate law-enforcement duties. A badge does not place a deputy above the law or exempt them from server rules and department procedures.

1.6

Respect for the Community

Deputies must treat civilians, suspects, witnesses, medical personnel and fellow law-enforcement officers with respect. Firm roleplay is permitted, but unnecessary humiliation, harassment or deliberately disruptive behaviour is not acceptable.

1.7

Integrity and Honesty

Deputies must act honestly when preparing reports, presenting evidence and describing events. Evidence must never be fabricated, altered, hidden or deliberately misrepresented.

1.8

Conflicts of Interest

Deputies should not personally handle investigations involving close friends, relatives, business partners or individuals with whom they have a significant personal dispute. Where possible, the matter should be passed to another deputy or senior officer.

1.9

Following Department Procedures

All deputies are expected to understand and follow the Sheriff’s Office handbook. Not knowing a procedure does not excuse serious or repeated misconduct.

1.10

Accountability

Deputies are responsible for their actions and must be prepared to explain their decisions to department leadership. Mistakes should be acknowledged honestly so they can be corrected and learned from.

1.11

Conduct Between Deputies

Members of the Sheriff’s Office must support one another and maintain professional communication. Disagreements should be handled privately and respectfully rather than argued publicly in front of civilians or suspects.

1.12

Serious Misconduct

Abuse of authority, corruption without approval, evidence tampering, deliberate dishonesty or repeated disregard for department procedures may result in disciplinary action or removal from the Sheriff’s Office.
