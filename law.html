Chapter I

Oath and Department Conduct

“A badge is not worn for authority, but for responsibility.”
1.1

The Deputy’s Oath

Every sworn deputy accepts a duty to protect the people of Ash Creek, uphold the law fairly and serve the territory with integrity. The badge must never be used for personal gain, intimidation or revenge.

1.2

Professional Conduct

Deputies must conduct themselves with professionalism while on duty and while representing the Sheriff’s Office. Members are expected to remain calm, respectful and reasonable, even when dealing with difficult or hostile individuals.

1.3

Roleplay Comes First

Law enforcement exists to create meaningful roleplay for everyone involved. Deputies should focus on developing scenes, investigations and consequences rather than rushing toward arrests or trying to win every encounter.

1.4

Fair and Impartial Policing

All individuals must be treated fairly regardless of their character’s reputation, occupation, relationships or previous dealings with law enforcement. Personal disagreements must not influence official decisions.

1.5

Use of Authority

Deputies may only use the authority granted by their position for legitimate law-enforcement duties. A badge does not place a deputy above the law or exempt them from server rules and department procedures.

1.6

Respect for the Community

Deputies must treat civilians, suspects, witnesses, medical personnel and fellow law-enforcement officers with respect. Firm roleplay is permitted, but unnecessary humiliation, harassment or deliberately disruptive behaviour is not acceptable.

1.7

Integrity and Honesty

Deputies must act honestly when preparing reports, presenting evidence and describing events. Evidence must never be fabricated, altered, hidden or deliberately misrepresented.

1.8

Conflicts of Interest

Deputies should not personally handle investigations involving close friends, relatives, business partners or individuals with whom they have a significant personal dispute. Where possible, the matter should be passed to another deputy or senior officer.

1.9

Following Department Procedures

All deputies are expected to understand and follow the Sheriff’s Office handbook. Not knowing a procedure does not excuse serious or repeated misconduct.

1.10

Accountability

Deputies are responsible for their actions and must be prepared to explain their decisions to department leadership. Mistakes should be acknowledged honestly so they can be corrected and learned from.

1.11

Conduct Between Deputies

Members of the Sheriff’s Office must support one another and maintain professional communication. Disagreements should be handled privately and respectfully rather than argued publicly in front of civilians or suspects.

1.12

Serious Misconduct

Abuse of authority, corruption without approval, evidence tampering, deliberate dishonesty or repeated disregard for department procedures may result in disciplinary action or removal from the Sheriff’s Office.

Chapter II

Chain of Command

"Order is maintained when every deputy understands both their duty and their place within the department."
2.1

Purpose of the Chain of Command

The Chain of Command exists to ensure the Sheriff's Office operates efficiently, consistently and professionally. Deputies should understand who they report to, who is responsible for making decisions and how information flows throughout the department.

2.2

Respect for Rank

Deputies are expected to respect the authority and responsibilities of senior department members. Lawful instructions issued by supervisors should be followed promptly unless doing so would violate server rules or department policy.

2.3

Leadership Responsibilities

Supervisors are expected to lead by example through professionalism, fairness and sound judgement. Leadership is not simply authority, it carries the responsibility to support deputies, resolve issues and maintain department standards.

2.4

Following Orders

Deputies should carry out lawful orders to the best of their ability. If clarification is required, questions should be asked respectfully before carrying out the instruction whenever practical.

2.5

Reporting Concerns

If a deputy believes an order conflicts with department policy or server rules, they should respectfully raise their concerns with the issuing supervisor. Where appropriate, concerns may be referred to higher department leadership for review.

2.6

Operational Authority

During active incidents, the designated officer in command is responsible for coordinating deputies, assigning responsibilities and directing the overall response. Deputies should avoid issuing conflicting instructions or disrupting the command structure during ongoing operations.

2.7

Communication

Clear communication is essential to effective policing. Deputies should provide accurate information, acknowledge instructions and keep supervisors informed of significant developments during investigations and incidents.

2.8

Professional Disagreements

Differences of opinion are natural within any department. Operational disagreements should be discussed respectfully after an incident whenever possible, rather than argued publicly during active roleplay.

2.9

Responsibility and Accountability

Each deputy remains responsible for their own actions, regardless of who issued an instruction. Following an order does not remove the responsibility to act lawfully, professionally and within department policy.

2.10

Supporting Fellow Deputies

The Sheriff's Office functions as a single department rather than a collection of individuals. Deputies should support one another, share information when appropriate and work together to provide consistent law enforcement throughout the Territory of Ash Creek.

Chapter III

Duties of a Deputy

"The badge is not a symbol of privilege, but a promise to serve the Territory of Ash Creek."
3.1

Preservation of Peace

The primary duty of every deputy is to preserve the peace and protect the people of Ash Creek. Deputies should seek to prevent crime where possible and respond promptly to incidents requiring law enforcement assistance.

3.2

Upholding the Law

Deputies are responsible for enforcing the laws of the Territory fairly, consistently and without favour. Enforcement should always be based upon the evidence available and the circumstances presented during roleplay.

3.3

Community Engagement

Deputies are encouraged to maintain positive relationships with the community through regular patrols, conversation and visible presence. Law enforcement should not exist solely to respond to crime but also to build public confidence and encourage roleplay.

3.4

Patrol Duties

Deputies should actively patrol the territory, monitor areas of public activity and remain available to respond to calls for assistance. Patrols should be conducted professionally while remaining observant of suspicious activity and potential hazards.

3.5

Protection of Life

Preserving human life should remain a deputy's highest operational priority whenever circumstances allow. Deputies should make reasonable efforts to safeguard civilians, fellow deputies, suspects and medical personnel during all incidents.

3.6

Cooperation with Other Departments

Deputies are expected to work professionally alongside the Medical Department and any other recognised government agencies. Mutual respect and cooperation between departments contributes to realistic and enjoyable roleplay.

3.7

Investigation of Crime

Deputies should investigate reported offences thoroughly and fairly. Whenever practical, investigations should rely upon witness statements, physical evidence and logical reasoning rather than assumptions.

3.8

Report Writing

Deputies are responsible for accurately documenting arrests, investigations, significant incidents and the use of force where required by department policy. Reports should be factual, complete and free from personal opinion.

3.9

Maintenance of Equipment

Deputies are expected to maintain the equipment issued to them and use departmental resources responsibly. Official equipment should only be used for legitimate law enforcement purposes.

3.10

Professional Appearance

Deputies should maintain an appearance appropriate to their role while on duty. Uniforms, equipment and presentation should reflect the professionalism expected of the Sheriff's Office.

3.11

Continuous Improvement

Deputies are encouraged to develop their knowledge, improve their decision-making and learn from both training and operational experience. Constructive feedback from supervisors should be viewed as an opportunity for professional development.

3.12

Representation of the Sheriff's Office

Every deputy represents the Sheriff's Office whenever they are on duty. Actions taken by individual deputies influence public confidence in the department, and members should conduct themselves in a manner that reflects honourably upon the office they serve.

Chapter IV

Patrol Procedures

"A successful patrol is measured not by the number of arrests made, but by the peace maintained."
4.1

Beginning a Shift

Before commencing patrol, deputies shall ensure they are properly equipped, appropriately dressed in the approved uniform and operating a serviceable patrol vehicle where applicable. Deputies should confirm they are prepared to respond to incidents before announcing themselves available for duty.

4.2

Patrol Readiness

Deputies are expected to carry all equipment necessary to perform their duties safely and effectively. Any missing, damaged or defective equipment should be reported to a supervisor as soon as practical.

4.3

Area Patrol

Deputies should maintain a visible presence throughout the Territory of Ash Creek by conducting regular patrols of populated areas, businesses, roads and other locations where public interaction is likely. Patrol routes should vary where practical and should not focus exclusively on known criminal locations.

4.4

Observational Duties

Deputies should remain attentive throughout their patrol, observing traffic, suspicious behaviour, disturbances and hazards that may require law enforcement intervention. Situational awareness is one of the deputy's most valuable tools.

4.5

Public Interaction

Deputies are encouraged to engage positively with members of the public during routine patrols. Not every interaction should involve enforcement action. Friendly conversation, providing assistance and answering questions help build public confidence in the Sheriff's Office.

4.6

Responding to Incidents

When responding to a reported incident, deputies should consider the nature of the call, available information and any potential risks before arriving on scene. Upon arrival, deputies should assess the situation before taking enforcement action.

4.7

Officer Safety

Deputies should make reasonable efforts to ensure both their own safety and the safety of others throughout any patrol. When circumstances require additional assistance, deputies should request backup rather than placing themselves or others at unnecessary risk.

4.8

Use of Communication

Deputies should maintain professional and concise communication while on duty. Important developments, requests for assistance and changes in operational status should be communicated clearly to other deputies when appropriate.

4.9

Vehicle Operation

Department vehicles should be operated responsibly and only for legitimate law enforcement purposes. Deputies should obey applicable laws unless operational circumstances reasonably justify emergency driving. Reckless driving without operational necessity is prohibited.

4.10

Patrol Discretion

Deputies should exercise sound judgement when deciding whether education, warning, mediation or formal enforcement is the most appropriate response. Not every situation requires an arrest or citation, and deputies should seek outcomes that encourage realistic and enjoyable roleplay.

4.11

Concluding a Patrol

At the conclusion of a shift, deputies should ensure that any required reports have been completed, evidence properly secured and outstanding matters communicated to department leadership where necessary. Equipment and department property should be returned in good condition.

4.12

Professional Conduct During Patrol

Throughout every patrol, deputies shall remember that they represent the Sheriff's Office and the Territory of Ash Creek. Every decision, interaction and enforcement action should reflect professionalism, fairness and the standards established throughout this handbook.

Chapter V

Arrests and Detainment

"An arrest is among the greatest authorities entrusted to a deputy. It shall be exercised with fairness, restraint and respect for the law."
5.1

Lawful Authority to Arrest

A deputy may only make an arrest when there is sufficient lawful justification under the laws of the Territory of Ash Creek. Arrests should never be made based solely upon suspicion, personal opinion or out-of-character knowledge.

5.2

Establishing Probable Cause

Before placing an individual under arrest, a deputy should be satisfied that the available facts, witness statements and evidence reasonably support the belief that a criminal offence has been committed. Deputies should be prepared to explain the reasons for an arrest if questioned by supervisors or the courts.

5.3

Identification

Whenever practical, deputies should identify themselves as members of the Sheriff's Office before making an arrest. Individuals should be informed that they are under arrest and, where circumstances safely permit, be told the reason for their detention.

5.4

Use of Force During Arrest

Only the amount of force reasonably necessary to safely effect an arrest may be used. Once a subject has been restrained and no longer presents a threat or attempts to flee, deputies shall reduce the level of force accordingly.

5.5

Searching a Prisoner

Following a lawful arrest, deputies may conduct a search for weapons, evidence and items relevant to the investigation. Searches should be carried out professionally while respecting the dignity of the individual whenever practical.

5.6

Transportation of Prisoners

Prisoners shall be transported safely and securely. Deputies should take reasonable precautions to prevent escape, injury or unnecessary harm during transportation.

5.7

Treatment of Persons in Custody

Every person taken into custody shall be treated with professionalism and respect. Harassment, unnecessary intimidation, humiliation or excessive force are inconsistent with the standards of the Sheriff's Office.

5.8

Medical Attention

If an arrested individual is injured, becomes ill or requests medical assistance, deputies should arrange appropriate medical treatment as soon as reasonably practical. Preservation of life shall remain the priority.

5.9

Processing an Arrest

Deputies should complete all necessary documentation relating to the arrest, including any reports, evidence and charges required by department policy. Reports should accurately reflect the events that occurred and remain free from personal bias.

5.10

Evidence and Property

Property seized during an arrest shall be handled in accordance with department evidence procedures. Deputies are responsible for maintaining the integrity of any evidence collected throughout the arrest process.

5.11

Temporary Detainment

Deputies may temporarily detain individuals when reasonably necessary to investigate a reported incident, ensure officer safety or prevent interference with an active investigation. Temporary detention should last no longer than reasonably required to resolve the situation.

5.12

Professional Judgement

The purpose of an arrest is to uphold the law and protect the community—not to punish, embarrass or dominate another person. Deputies should always consider whether their actions contribute to fair, believable and enjoyable roleplay while remaining consistent with the law.

Chapter VI

The Use of Lawful Force

"The authority to use force is entrusted to deputies not to dominate others, but to protect life, preserve peace and uphold the law."
6.1

Purpose of Force

Deputies may use force only when it is reasonably necessary to protect themselves, protect others, prevent the escape of a lawful detainee or carry out a lawful arrest. Whenever possible, situations should be resolved through communication and voluntary compliance before force becomes necessary.

6.2

Necessity

Force shall only be used when lesser alternatives are ineffective, unavailable or would reasonably place the deputy or another person at unnecessary risk. Deputies should continually assess whether force remains necessary throughout an incident.

6.3

Reasonable and Proportionate Force

Any force used must be reasonable, proportionate and appropriate to the threat presented. Deputies should always use the minimum level of force reasonably necessary to safely resolve the situation.

6.4

De-escalation

Deputies should make reasonable efforts to de-escalate situations through communication, patience and sound judgement whenever circumstances safely permit. Successful law enforcement is often achieved through calm decision-making rather than physical confrontation.

6.5

Preservation of Life

The preservation of human life shall remain the highest priority during any use-of-force incident. Deputies should take reasonable steps to protect civilians, fellow deputies, suspects and medical personnel from unnecessary harm.

6.6

Escalation and De-escalation of Force

The level of force used should increase only when the actions of an individual reasonably justify it. Likewise, once resistance or the threat has diminished, deputies shall reduce or discontinue the use of force as soon as it is safe to do so.

6.7

Deadly Force

Deadly force may only be used when a deputy reasonably believes it is immediately necessary to protect themselves or another person from an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm. Deadly force shall never be used solely to punish, intimidate or gain compliance.

6.8

Persons in Custody

Once an individual has been restrained and no longer presents an immediate threat or attempts to escape, deputies shall cease using force except where reasonably necessary to maintain control. Persons in custody shall be treated professionally and with respect.

6.9

Medical Assistance

Following any significant use of force, deputies should ensure injured persons receive appropriate medical attention as soon as circumstances reasonably allow. Deputies should cooperate fully with responding medical personnel.

6.10

Accountability

Any significant use of force shall be accurately documented in accordance with department reporting procedures. Deputies must be prepared to explain why force was necessary and why the level of force used was reasonable under the circumstances.

6.11

Prohibited Conduct

Deputies shall not use force: As punishment. Out of anger, frustration or retaliation. To intimidate or humiliate another person. After an individual has surrendered and no longer presents a threat. In any manner that is clearly excessive or unnecessary. Violations of these standards may result in disciplinary action.

6.12

Professional Judgement

Every use-of-force incident is unique and must be assessed according to the circumstances known to the deputy at the time. Deputies are expected to exercise restraint, sound judgement and professionalism in every encounter, remembering that the ultimate objective is to resolve incidents safely while maintaining public trust in the Sheriff's Office.

Chapter VII

Investigations and Warrants

"Justice is best served when facts are gathered carefully, evidence is preserved faithfully and conclusions are reached without prejudice."
7.1

Purpose of an Investigation

The purpose of every investigation is to establish the facts surrounding a reported offence and determine whether a crime has occurred. Deputies should remain impartial throughout an investigation and avoid forming conclusions before sufficient evidence has been obtained.

7.2

Professional Conduct

Deputies shall conduct investigations with honesty, patience and professionalism. All individuals involved in an investigation, including victims, witnesses and suspects, should be treated with fairness and respect.

7.3

Securing the Scene

When arriving at the location of a suspected crime, deputies should make reasonable efforts to secure the area, protect those present and preserve potential evidence. Unnecessary disturbance of the scene should be avoided whenever practical.

7.4

Collection of Evidence

Evidence should be collected carefully and documented accurately. Deputies should preserve the integrity of physical evidence and avoid contamination or unnecessary handling. Evidence must never be fabricated, altered, concealed or intentionally destroyed.

7.5

Witness Statements

Witnesses should be interviewed respectfully and allowed to provide their account without unnecessary interruption. Deputies should record relevant information as accurately as possible while recognising that witness accounts may differ.

7.6

Interviewing Suspects

Suspects should be questioned professionally and without coercion. Deputies should avoid threats, intimidation or promises intended solely to obtain a confession. The objective of an interview is to establish the truth, not simply to obtain an admission of guilt.

7.7

Developing Probable Cause

Before making an arrest or requesting a warrant, deputies should ensure that the available evidence reasonably supports their belief that an offence has been committed. Investigations should be based upon facts and logical reasoning rather than assumptions or personal opinion.

7.8

Search Warrants

Where required by law, deputies shall seek a warrant before searching private property or seizing evidence. A warrant application should clearly state: The offence being investigated. The facts establishing probable cause. The location to be searched. The evidence sought. Deputies shall execute warrants only within the authority granted by the issuing court.

7.9

Arrest Warrants

An arrest warrant may be sought when there is sufficient evidence to establish probable cause that a named individual has committed a criminal offence and immediate arrest is not practical or appropriate. Deputies should ensure warrant applications accurately reflect the available evidence.

7.10

Confidentiality

Information obtained during an investigation should only be shared with individuals who have a legitimate operational need to know. Deputies should avoid publicly discussing active investigations or revealing confidential information without proper authority.

7.11

Documentation

Investigations should be documented thoroughly through reports, evidence logs and witness statements. Accurate documentation ensures accountability and allows future review of investigative decisions.

7.12

Integrity of the Investigation

The credibility of the Sheriff's Office depends upon the integrity of its investigations. Deputies shall conduct every investigation impartially, follow the evidence wherever it leads and remain committed to discovering the truth rather than proving a predetermined conclusion.

Chapter VIII

Reports and Evidence

"A deputy's memory may fade, but a truthful report preserves the facts for those who follow."
8.1

Purpose of Reports

Reports serve as the official record of law enforcement activity within the Territory of Ash Creek. They provide an accurate account of incidents, arrests, investigations and other significant events, ensuring transparency and accountability.

8.2

Accuracy

All reports shall be truthful, complete and based upon the deputy's observations, evidence and information reasonably available at the time. Deputies shall not knowingly include false, misleading or exaggerated information in any official report.

8.3

Timeliness

Reports should be completed as soon as reasonably practical following an incident while the details remain fresh and accurate. Unnecessary delays should be avoided unless operational duties require immediate attention elsewhere.

8.4

Professional Writing

Reports should be written clearly, objectively and professionally. Deputies should avoid personal opinions, assumptions or emotionally charged language unless directly relevant to the incident. The report should present the facts rather than personal conclusions.

8.5

Required Documentation

Whenever applicable, reports should include: The date and time of the incident. The location of the incident. The names of deputies involved. The identities of victims, witnesses and suspects where known. A chronological summary of events. Any evidence collected. Any enforcement action taken. Any injuries or medical treatment provided.

8.6

Evidence Handling

Evidence collected during an investigation shall be handled carefully to preserve its integrity. Deputies should document where evidence was found, how it was obtained and maintain appropriate control of it until it is properly submitted or secured.

8.7

Preservation of Evidence

Evidence must be protected from loss, contamination, alteration or unnecessary handling. Deputies shall never fabricate, destroy, conceal or intentionally tamper with evidence. Any action that compromises the integrity of evidence may undermine an investigation and damage public confidence in the Sheriff's Office.

8.8

Photographs and Documentation

Where appropriate, deputies should document scenes through photographs, sketches or written descriptions. Such documentation should accurately reflect the scene as it existed at the time of the investigation.

8.9

Supplemental Reports

If new information becomes available after an initial report has been submitted, deputies should prepare a supplemental report rather than altering the original record. Maintaining a complete history of an investigation promotes accountability and transparency.

8.10

Confidentiality of Records

Official reports, investigative documents and evidence shall be treated as confidential departmental records. Deputies shall only share such information with authorised persons who have a legitimate operational or legal need to access it.

8.11

Accountability

Every deputy is personally responsible for the accuracy of reports they prepare and the evidence they collect. Deputies should be prepared to explain and justify the contents of any report they submit.

8.12

Integrity Above All

The credibility of the Sheriff's Office depends upon honest reporting and trustworthy evidence. A deputy's responsibility is not to secure convictions or justify arrests, but to faithfully document the truth as it occurred. Accurate records ensure that justice is based upon facts, preserve public confidence in the department and protect the integrity of every investigation.
