Chapter I

Physician's Oath and Conduct

“The physician's calling is to preserve life, relieve suffering and serve the people of Ash Creek with skill, compassion and honour.”
1.1

The Physician's Oath

Every member of the Ash Creek Medical Department accepts the responsibility to provide medical care to those in need, preserve life whenever reasonably possible and uphold the highest standards of professional conduct. Medical knowledge shall be used to heal, never to cause unnecessary harm.

1.2

Professional Conduct

Medical personnel shall conduct themselves with professionalism, courtesy and respect while representing the Medical Department. Patients, colleagues and members of the public shall be treated with dignity regardless of their background, occupation or personal circumstances.

1.3

Patient Welfare

The health and wellbeing of the patient shall remain the primary concern of every physician. Medical decisions should always be guided by the patient's condition and best interests.

1.4

Impartial Treatment

Medical care shall be provided without discrimination or favour. A patient's reputation, criminal history, social standing or personal relationships shall not influence the quality of care they receive. Every individual is entitled to appropriate medical treatment.

1.5

Preservation of Life

Whenever reasonably possible, physicians should make every effort to preserve life and prevent unnecessary suffering. Medical personnel should exercise sound judgement when determining the most appropriate course of treatment for each patient.

1.6

Professional Judgement

Physicians are expected to exercise their own medical judgement based upon the patient's condition, available medical knowledge and the circumstances presented. Medical decisions should never be influenced by personal bias or external pressure.

1.7

Confidentiality

Information obtained while treating a patient shall be regarded as confidential. Medical personnel should only disclose patient information when required by law, necessary for patient care or authorised by department policy.

1.8

Cooperation with Other Departments

Medical personnel shall work professionally alongside the Sheriff's Office and other recognised government agencies. While cooperation is encouraged, physicians remain responsible for making independent medical decisions in the best interests of their patients.

1.9

Professional Appearance

Members of the Medical Department should maintain an appearance appropriate to their duties. Uniforms, equipment and personal presentation should reflect the professionalism expected of Ash Creek physicians.

1.10

Continuing Education

Medicine is a profession that requires continual learning. Medical personnel are encouraged to improve their knowledge, refine their skills and participate in departmental training whenever opportunities arise.

1.11

Conduct Within the Department

Medical personnel shall treat fellow members of the department with professionalism and respect. Disagreements should be resolved constructively and should never interfere with patient care or department operations.

1.12

Integrity of the Profession

The trust placed in the Medical Department is earned through honesty, compassion and professional excellence. Every physician represents not only themselves but the reputation of the department as a whole. Members shall strive to uphold the confidence of the community by acting with integrity, treating every patient fairly and remaining faithful to the principles of their profession.

Chapter II

Department Structure

“A well-organized medical department ensures that every patient receives timely, coordinated and professional care.”
2.1

Purpose of the Department

The Ash Creek Medical Department exists to provide emergency medical care, preserve life, promote public health and support the wellbeing of the Territory of Ash Creek. Every member of the department contributes to these objectives through professionalism, teamwork and medical excellence.

2.2

Department Organization

The Medical Department operates through a structured chain of leadership designed to ensure efficient decision-making, effective communication and consistent standards of patient care. Every member should understand their responsibilities and the authority of those appointed to supervise departmental operations.

2.3

Respect for Leadership

Medical personnel shall respect the authority and responsibilities of department leadership. Lawful instructions issued by supervisors should be followed promptly unless doing so would conflict with department policy, medical ethics or server rules.

2.4

Responsibilities of Supervisors

Supervisors are expected to provide leadership through professionalism, fairness and sound clinical judgement. They are responsible for supporting staff, maintaining departmental standards, resolving operational issues and ensuring quality patient care. Leadership carries the responsibility to guide others, not simply to exercise authority.

2.5

Clinical Decision-Making

While department leadership oversees operations, medical personnel remain responsible for exercising independent professional judgement when treating patients. Clinical decisions should always prioritize the patient's condition and medical needs.

2.6

Communication

Clear and professional communication is essential to effective patient care. Medical personnel should keep colleagues informed of significant developments, patient status and operational needs while maintaining patient confidentiality whenever appropriate.

2.7

Teamwork

Medical care is a collaborative effort. Members of the department should assist one another whenever practical, share relevant information and work together to ensure patients receive safe and effective treatment.

2.8

Cooperation with Other Departments

The Medical Department regularly works alongside the Sheriff's Office and other recognised government agencies. Professional cooperation is encouraged; however, medical personnel remain responsible for making independent clinical decisions without improper influence from outside agencies.

2.9

Raising Concerns

Members who believe a decision, instruction or departmental practice conflicts with medical standards or department policy should respectfully raise their concerns through the appropriate chain of command. Constructive discussion strengthens the department and promotes high standards of patient care.

2.10

Accountability

Every member of the Medical Department is accountable for their own professional decisions and actions. Following the instructions of another staff member does not remove the responsibility to act ethically, professionally and within department policy.

2.11

Professional Conduct Within the Department

Medical personnel should foster a workplace built upon mutual respect, trust and professionalism. Disagreements should be resolved respectfully and privately whenever possible and should never compromise patient care.

2.12

Commitment to the Department

The strength of the Medical Department depends upon every member fulfilling their role with competence, integrity and dedication. By working together within a clear department structure, medical personnel ensure that the people of Ash Creek receive consistent, professional and compassionate care whenever it is needed.

Chapter III

Patient Assessment

“A careful assessment is the foundation of every successful treatment. Before a physician can heal, they must first understand.”
3.1

Purpose of Patient Assessment

Patient assessment is the process of evaluating an individual's condition to determine the nature and severity of illness or injury. Every assessment should be conducted methodically, professionally and with the objective of providing the most appropriate medical care.

3.2

Scene Safety

Before approaching a patient, medical personnel shall ensure the scene is reasonably safe for themselves, the patient and others present. If an active threat exists, assistance from the Sheriff's Office should be requested before treatment begins whenever practical. Medical personnel should never unnecessarily place themselves in danger.

3.3

Initial Assessment

Upon reaching the patient, medical personnel should perform an initial assessment to identify any immediate threats to life. Priority should be given to identifying conditions requiring urgent intervention before conducting a more detailed examination.

3.4

Primary Survey

The primary survey should focus on identifying and addressing life-threatening conditions. Medical personnel should assess: Airway. Breathing. Circulation. Level of consciousness. Severe bleeding or other immediate dangers. Life-threatening problems should be addressed as soon as they are identified.

3.5

Secondary Assessment

Once immediate threats have been managed, a more comprehensive examination should be performed. This assessment should include the patient's symptoms, medical history where available, visible injuries and any additional findings relevant to diagnosis and treatment.

3.6

Communication with the Patient

Whenever the patient's condition permits, medical personnel should communicate clearly and respectfully throughout the assessment. Patients should be informed of what is being examined and encouraged to describe their symptoms, pain and any events leading to their injury or illness.

3.7

Clinical Observation

Physicians should rely upon careful observation in addition to physical examination. Changes in appearance, behaviour, responsiveness or vital signs may provide important information regarding the patient's condition. Medical decisions should be based upon objective findings whenever possible.

3.8

Ongoing Assessment

Patient assessment does not conclude after the initial examination. Medical personnel should continually reassess the patient's condition throughout treatment and transport, recognising that injuries and illnesses may change over time. Treatment plans should be adjusted whenever new information becomes available.

3.9

Working with Other Responders

When multiple responders are present, medical personnel should communicate findings clearly to ensure coordinated patient care. Relevant information should be shared with assisting medical staff and, where appropriate, with law enforcement while respecting patient confidentiality.

3.10

Professional Judgement

Every patient presents unique circumstances. Medical personnel are expected to apply sound clinical judgement, adapt their assessment to the situation and avoid making assumptions before sufficient information has been obtained.

3.11

Documentation of Findings

Significant observations made during the assessment should be accurately documented as part of the patient's medical record. Clear documentation supports continuity of care, accountability and future medical decision-making.

3.12

Compassionate Care

Patient assessment is not solely a clinical process but also an opportunity to provide reassurance and build trust. Medical personnel should approach every patient with compassion, patience and professionalism, recognising that calm and respectful care is often as valuable as medical treatment itself.

Chapter IV

Emergency Response

“The duty of a physician is to preserve life whenever possible.”
4.1

Purpose of Emergency Attendance

Members of the Ash Creek Medical Department are entrusted with rendering aid to those suffering from illness, injury or other sudden afflictions requiring immediate medical attention. Every emergency should be approached with urgency, composure and sound medical judgement.

4.2

Receiving Word of an Emergency

Medical assistance may be requested by messenger, telegraph, members of the Sheriff's Office or concerned citizens. Upon receiving word of an emergency, the attending physician should obtain as much information as reasonably possible concerning the patient's condition, the location of the incident and any dangers that may be present before departing.

4.3

Travelling to the Scene

Physicians shall proceed to the patient without unnecessary delay, travelling by horseback, wagon or other suitable means available to the department. While haste is often necessary, neither physician nor mount should be placed in unnecessary danger through reckless travel.

4.4

Arrival at the Scene

Upon arrival, the attending physician shall first determine whether the scene is reasonably safe before rendering aid. Where violence, fire, unstable structures or other hazards remain present, assistance from the Sheriff's Office should be requested before treatment begins whenever practical. A physician who becomes injured cannot render aid to others.

4.5

Examination of the Injured

The attending physician should conduct an orderly examination of each patient to determine the nature and severity of their injuries or illness. Immediate attention shall be given to conditions threatening life, including severe bleeding, difficulty breathing or unconsciousness. When several patients require assistance, those in greatest medical need should receive treatment first.

4.6

Rendering Medical Aid

Following examination, treatment should be administered according to the physician's knowledge, experience and the resources available. The physician should endeavour to preserve life, relieve suffering and prevent further deterioration until more comprehensive care may be provided.

4.7

Cooperation with the Sheriff's Office

Physicians and deputies frequently serve together during accidents, disturbances and criminal investigations. Members of both departments shall work cooperatively while respecting the distinct responsibilities of each profession. Medical decisions concerning the care of a patient shall remain the responsibility of the attending physician.

4.8

Conveyance of the Patient

Should the patient's condition require continued care beyond what may reasonably be provided at the scene, arrangements should be made to convey the patient to the Ash Creek Hospital. Transport may be accomplished by horse-drawn wagon, carriage, improvised litter or any other practical means suitable to the patient's condition and the surrounding circumstances. Care should be taken to minimise unnecessary discomfort throughout the journey.

4.9

Incidents Involving Multiple Casualties

Where several persons are injured, physicians should organise their efforts to ensure that those in greatest need receive immediate attention. Available personnel, equipment and supplies should be employed wisely until additional assistance arrives. Calm organisation is essential during times of great confusion.

4.10

Record of Treatment

As soon as circumstances reasonably permit, the attending physician should record the patient's condition, the treatment administered and any significant observations made during the course of care. Such records provide an accurate account of the physician's attendance and support future treatment.

4.11

Conduct During Emergencies

Medical emergencies are often accompanied by fear, confusion and grief. Physicians should remain composed, speak with confidence and conduct themselves in a manner that reassures both patients and those gathered nearby. Professional conduct inspires confidence in the Medical Department and assists in the orderly management of emergencies.

4.12

Duty to the Territory

Every member of the Medical Department shall meet that trust with courage, compassion and unwavering dedication, remembering that no journey is too long and no hour too late when a life may yet be preserved.

Chapter V

Treatment Standards

“The art of healing lies not only in knowledge, but in the careful application of that knowledge for the benefit of the sick and injured.”
5.1

Purpose of Treatment

The object of medical treatment is to preserve life where possible, relieve suffering and restore the patient to good health. Every physician shall endeavour to provide care according to the best medical knowledge available and the circumstances before them.

5.2

Examination Before Treatment

Except where immediate action is necessary to preserve life, no treatment should be undertaken until the physician has completed a careful examination of the patient. Sound medical judgement begins with proper observation rather than assumption.

5.3

Cleanliness

Physicians shall maintain the highest standard of cleanliness reasonably possible during the treatment of patients. Medical instruments, dressings and the physician's hands should be cleaned before use whenever circumstances permit, in order to reduce the risk of infection and promote successful recovery.

5.4

Dressings and Bandages

Wounds should be cleaned and dressed with suitable bandages or cloths appropriate to the injury. Dressings should be secured in a manner that protects the wound while avoiding unnecessary discomfort or restriction. Patients should be instructed to keep dressings clean and return should further attention be required.

5.5

Fractures and Broken Bones

Suspected fractures should be carefully examined before movement. Where practical, broken limbs should be supported with splints or other suitable materials to prevent further injury before the patient is conveyed for continued care. Unnecessary movement of the injured shall be avoided.

5.6

Medicines and Tonics

Medicinal preparations shall be administered only when the attending physician believes they are likely to benefit the patient. Physicians should exercise restraint in the use of powerful medicines, recognising that many remedies provide comfort when properly used but may cause harm if administered carelessly.

5.7

Surgical Procedures

When an injury or illness requires surgical intervention, the physician shall ensure that the procedure is necessary and that reasonable preparations have been made beforehand. Operations should be conducted with due care, suitable instruments and every effort made to preserve the patient's life throughout the procedure.

5.8

Relief of Pain

The relief of suffering is an important duty of the physician. Where appropriate, medicines intended to lessen pain or permit necessary treatment may be administered according to the physician's judgement, while exercising caution in their use.

5.9

Continuing Care

Recovery seldom concludes with the first treatment. Patients should receive further examination whenever necessary to observe their progress, change dressings, adjust treatment or address any complications that may arise.

5.10

Limits of Medical Knowledge

No physician possesses perfect knowledge. When confronted with an unfamiliar illness or particularly grave injury, members of the Medical Department are encouraged to consult one another so that the patient may benefit from their combined experience. Humility in medicine is a strength rather than a weakness.

5.11

Care and Stewardship of Medical Supplies

Medicines, surgical instruments and other medical equipment entrusted to the Medical Department shall be maintained in proper condition and used responsibly. Supplies should be conserved where practical without compromising the care afforded to patients.

5.12

Duty of the Physician

Every treatment reflects the honour of the Medical Department and the trust placed in its physicians. A physician shall perform their duties with diligence, compassion and integrity, remembering that careful judgement, steady hands and a kind manner are often the greatest remedies that can be offered to those in need.

Chapter VI

Death and Incapacitation

“Though every physician strives to preserve life, there are occasions when nature prevails. In such moments, the physician's duty is to act with dignity, honesty and respect for both the living and the deceased.”
6.1

Preservation of Life

The preservation of life shall remain the foremost duty of every physician. No patient should be declared deceased until every reasonable effort has been made to examine their condition and provide appropriate treatment, where such treatment is likely to be of benefit.

6.2

Examination of the Patient

Before determining that life has departed, the attending physician shall conduct a careful examination of the patient. The physician should satisfy themselves that there are no reasonable signs indicating that recovery remains possible before reaching such a conclusion.

6.3

Incapacitated Patients

Patients who are unconscious or otherwise incapable of speaking for themselves shall continue to receive treatment according to the physician's best medical judgement. Medical decisions should always be made in the interests of the patient's wellbeing until they recover or their condition otherwise becomes clear.

6.4

Pronouncement of Death

The determination that a patient has died shall rest solely with the attending physician. Such a determination should never be made carelessly or without due examination, recognising the gravity of the decision and its consequences.

6.5

Care of the Deceased

Those who have passed from this life shall be treated with the same dignity and respect afforded to the living. The body should be handled carefully, protected from unnecessary disturbance and prepared for release to the proper civil authority or the next of kin where appropriate.

6.6

Communication with Family

Where family members or close companions are present, physicians should communicate with compassion, honesty and dignity. News of a patient's passing should be delivered calmly and respectfully, recognising the grief experienced by those left behind.

6.7

Cooperation with the Sheriff's Office

Where a death appears to have resulted from violence, criminal conduct or suspicious circumstances, the attending physician should cooperate fully with the Sheriff's Office. Care should be taken to avoid unnecessarily disturbing the deceased or any surrounding evidence until deputies have completed their lawful duties.

6.8

Professional Conduct

Physicians should conduct themselves with composure and discretion following the death of a patient. Speculation concerning the cause of death or discussion of private medical matters should be avoided except where required in the course of official duties.

6.9

Respect for the Profession

The manner in which a physician conducts themselves following the death of a patient reflects upon both the Medical Department and the profession itself. Every physician should perform these duties with humility, recognising that death is a solemn part of medical practice.

6.10

Acceptance of Nature's Course

Medicine cannot overcome every illness nor heal every wound. Physicians should never regard the death of a patient as a personal failure when every reasonable effort has been made according to their knowledge, skill and the circumstances before them. Instead, they should remain steadfast in their calling and continue to serve those whose lives may yet be preserved.
